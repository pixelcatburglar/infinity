# Features

- **Faction warfare information.** Corporation militia whitelists remain available, but direct enlistment and leaving are refused while faction warfare is closed. Empty occupation, victory-point and shipcaster information lets the dashboard open; as server policy, faction home systems from static data serve as the displayed headquarters locations, not official warzone HQ data.
- **Achievement definitions.** The server verifies and retains the retail achievement catalogue at startup and serves its unchanged CDN bytes with a matching precomputed checkpoint. Server integrity policy requires increasing positive milestone targets, positive reward amounts, valid contribution declarations and ordered dates; achievement-typed matcher values must reference existing achievements without cycles. Other matcher values remain opaque. Achievement progress, event producers, rewards and title ownership are not implemented by the catalogue.
- **Daily goals.** The backend uses deterministic, original server-authored Lua and JSON content, not a reproduction of official server tables. For each character and UTC day it chooses one ordinary goal from each of three disjoint pools and a fourth from the remaining union, plus a daily bonus; each UTC month has four base and four Omega milestones. Issued definitions and rewards are frozen. The first activation applies to its current periods; later publications take effect independently on the next UTC day and month. The durable shared namespace activation history preserves returns such as A → B → A. Completed, unclaimed rights remain redeemable permanently; offline characters receive no grants or missed offline periods, and get current periods on return.
  - Natural contributions cover NPC kills, ship damage, ore mining, signature scanning, manufacturing, wreck salvage, loyalty-point earnings, space jumps, remote shield repair and remote armor repair, from canonical committed sources. Damage and repair retain exact binary HP amounts and fractional progress. Completion-derived counters use the same typed match: ordinary goal UUID and category within the day, and bonus UUID and category within the monthly window, counting distinct dates. First completion and EverMark reward writes emit closed `DailyGoalCompleted` and `EverMarksEarned` facts, not duplicate callbacks or recursive subscriptions. Faction-warfare contribution schemas are recognized but cannot activate into playable pools without a producer.
  - Completing a current ordinary goal with EverMarks costs 2,500 LP for the first paid completion and 4,500 for the second. The remaining allowance is two minus the greater of natural bonus progress and paid count. Payment, pending progress and completion settle atomically, without redeeming. If the internal payment plan exceeds 13 pending deliveries or 13 affected goals, it refuses with 403 without debit or draining; normal delivery can drain the backlog before retry.
  - Redeeming eligible frozen rewards atomically credits ISK, EverMarks, free skill points, items or PLEX as applicable. Items go to the selected current or home NPC station's personal hangar root; an invalid destination is refused. Daily ISK is tax-free. Omega rights earned while Omega survive downgrade; rewards earned while Alpha can unlock on a later upgrade. PLEX audit references use the existing server-only sequence, not a UUID ledger redesign. Retained reads and claims use their original immutable policy rather than reauthorizing against current static data.
  - The shipped values are in `crates/server/lua/content/daily_goals/data/daily_goals.json`: ordinary goals award 500 EverMarks, the daily bonus requires two ordinary completions and awards 10,000 free SP, and monthly thresholds are 3, 6, 9 and 12 bonus days. These are this server's authored values, not client-derived official rewards. Backend implementation does not enable the reserved client UI release gates; see [limitations](limitations.md) and [remote services](remote-services.md#daily-goals).
- **Sovereignty Hubs.** An eligible Hub can be deployed as a neutral physical structure and contested by independent alliances using Entosis links. A full paid warm-up cycle precedes scoring; subsequent cycles use effective dogma fuel, capacitor, duration and range. Links of one alliance do not stack speed; opposing alliances block progress, and control reverses through zero. A successful neutral claim commits the corporation asset and alliance sovereignty together. The first eligible claim establishes an alliance's current capital immediately and starts its appointment cooldown; loss of that capital clears it and extends the cooldown. Same-alliance corporation transfer preserves the claim, while destruction and alliance departure can remove it. Self-destruct may be cancelled; restart recovery cancels unfinished requests.
  - System, map, station and alliance reads use committed ownership and history. A neutral Hub remains a physical structure in lists, but the legacy claimed-Hub information read returns `None` until claimed. Corporation and alliance notifications are server recipient policy: all nondeleted members of the affected corporation or alliance, including offline members, receive the applicable persistent notices. Initial neutral capture produces a real acquisition, not an SCC loss or destruction; losing a current capital publishes the cache update without a false permanent capital-change notice.
  - An active link uses the shared warp, jump, dock and cloak gates. Capital remote assistance is restricted while subcapital assistance remains permitted. Capital cycle duration uses effective SDE passive modifiers, not a hardcoded multiplier. Capture checkpoints retain contributor history for organization invalidation; under bounded history pressure unresolved progress freezes rather than being accepted without proof. A restart drops active links and does not accrue offline capture.

- **Login and characters.** GPS handshake with the Placebo crypto pack. An account is created the first time a new user name logs in. Character selection and creation. Each account has a clone grade, Alpha or Omega, kept in the database. Accounts start as Omega with no end date; a GM can change the grade with `/clonegrade`, and an Omega grade given for a set time turns into Alpha when its grace period runs out, also across a restart. A character with no active ship, such as a new one, receives their race's rookie ship fitted with online civilian modules (a gun, a miner, an afterburner and defensive modules) and one Civilian Hobgoblin in the drone bay. The server chooses this fit from the civilian module line; it is not a copy of the live server's. Civilian guns need no ammunition. An existing ship is never refitted or restocked.
  - At an NPC station, a pilot outside a rookie ship can independently request a racial rookie replacement, keeping the old ship and its contents. Once per character, a separate request grants the six entries of the shared server-selected racial fit loose in the hangar. The actual-client buttons and hangar refresh are not verified.
- **Stations and items.** Inventory and hangars, and the assets window with asset search. An item can be moved only where the pilot can reach both ends: when docked, the current station's hangars, the pilot's corporation office there, and the ships and containers inside them; in space, the active ship, an accessible nearby fleet hangar or ship maintenance bay, and a cargo container or wreck within its operational distance. Every space check (hangar containers, cargo and other holds, contracts and courier mission cargo) counts an item at the volume the client shows: a packaged ship or module takes its packaged volume, and a courier package takes the volume of what it holds. Boarding, assembling and repackaging ships; a sealed courier package cannot be repackaged. Repackaging a fitted ship puts its modules, subsystems, drones, cargo and ammunition in the station hangar; rigs cannot be unfitted, so they are destroyed once the player confirms the client's warning, except on strategic cruisers, whose rigs go to the hangar too. Saved fittings, ship insurance, jump clones and reprocessing. Station repair: the quote covers the armor, hull and module damage of the selected items, and the ISK is paid in the same transaction as the repair. A selection without modules can be repaired in part; one that includes a module must be paid in full. Docking does not repair a ship. Private notes in the info window. The station guest list shows the other online pilots docked in the same station and updates as they arrive or leave. Crystals show the damage they have taken. A new name for a ship, container or other item shows at once wherever it is listed. Trashing removes an item together with everything inside it in one step; online modules are taken offline first, which also clears their weapon groups, pending reloads and overheating. Items in a corporation's Deliveries hangar cannot be trashed.
  - Inventory inspection reads direct contents of authorized ships and containers, including owned assembled ships and containers at their supported remote station or system root. It checks current ownership, corporation custody and query authority, or applicable shared-bay grants and reachability. Fully authorized ship inspection also includes loaded charges; a shared bay does not expose the host's private fittings or charges. Container opening validation compares submitted inventory row snapshots against current local access and returns invalid item IDs; malformed input is refused. These reads do not change inventory or load fittings.
  - Ship storage covers the client-defined cargo hold, drone and fighter bays, ship maintenance bay, fleet hangar and specialized holds. Each hold uses its effective dogma capacity, including fitting and skill modifiers, and its client-defined item acceptance rules; a ship without a hold cannot use it. Inventory writes reject items that are not accepted or do not fit, without changing the items. The frigate escape bay has one slot. When docked, an assembled ship other than the active ship or a capsule can be moved by ordinary inventory movement into an accepting ship hold, including a maintenance bay, and back; its contents travel with it.
  - Ordinary cargo, secure, audit-log secure and freight containers have their own static capacity: the SDE capacity attribute, then type capacity, then zero. Skills, fitting and instance attributes do not modify it, and parent quantity does not multiply it. The server's capacity reply and admission count each direct child's own packaged, assembled or courier-wrap volume across all owners and flags, plus unresolved reload reservations sourced there; nested contents are not recursively added. That reply and admission share the capacity resolver and volume aggregation, and the reply uses a coherent snapshot. Existing access, reachability and singleton gates remain unchanged. Zero is not unlimited: a zero-capacity or already overfull container permits equal or decreasing organization, extraction, flag changes, splitting, merging and restacking, but not growth. Moves, deposits, unloads, generated inputs and mutaplasmid creation check the actual destination and refuse atomically without partial publication. Crate rewards retain their existing station or ship destination, not the source box.
  - Inventory quantities and each requested quantity use signed 64-bit integers. When an eligible stack lacks room, a deposit fills it and creates a second stack for the remainder; a full stack can be bypassed. The business operation still occurs once, with source changes and all resulting rows and notifications committed together.
  - A ship owner can independently allow fleet and corporation access to each of the fleet hangar and ship maintenance bay. All four settings default to off and belong to the current hull owner; a new owner starts with disabled settings. In space, access requires the matching current fleet or corporation membership and switch, a visible ship in the same system and a sampled surface distance of at most 2,500 m. The owner passes the sharing check but still needs physical and legal access. A disconnected member's reserved fleet seat is not current membership. The server checks current authority on each operation, not the client's cached settings.
  - Physical hull dirt timestamps persist across owner transfers and reset only on an authorized cleaning request. An owned active or inactive ship in the current NPC station hangar, or the current piloted hull in space, can be reset; visual reads also allow visible foreign hulls in space. Real clean-button UI behavior remains unverified.
  - An authorized bay lists contents of every owner, and its effective capacity includes all contents and reserved reload charges. Deposits, including stored ships and their contents, retain their owners; they are not gifts to the host. Taking a whole item out of the bay or into the taker's private ship transfers the root and all descendants to the taker, even when the root was already theirs. Moving within the same bay through ordinary containers retains ownership; moving to a different bay is a take. Ordinary containers inherit bay access, but each ship ancestor must permit access: a shared maintenance bay does not open a foreign stored ship's private cargo or fittings, even to the owner of an item inside. Cargo, fittings, drone, fighter, fuel and other specialized holds remain private. Capacity, item acceptance, fitting, quantity, trade and industry restrictions still apply.
  - Labels require the item's owner or applicable corporation authority; take a guest-owned item first before renaming it. Crystal damage queries can include currently visible guest items. Consuming an expert system from a shared stack takes only the consumed quantity for the actor's license; the guest-owned remainder stays with its owner. A manual reload likewise takes the reserved quantity for the acting pilot, while the untaken guest stack remains guest-owned. If interrupted, the charges return to an eligible actor-owned source or the actor's weapon ship cargo, not to the guest; if a refund cannot fit, the reservation remains and the ownership change rolls back. Source owners and authorized viewers receive appropriate bay changes, without exposing private refund or license contents.
  - In space, `StoreVessel` stores the selected legal assembled non-capsule ship in an accessible maintenance bay and puts its pilot in a new capsule. `BoardStoredShip` takes an eligible assembled ship from that exact bay, subject to skills and occupancy checks, and leaves the previous active ship unpiloted in space. `LaunchFromContainer` takes such a ship into space unpiloted without changing the selected ship. The operations check current scope, authority, range, warp state, capacity and legal item state; ownership and pending reloads are settled atomically. Store and board use the fenced boarding settlement and publication path; launch uses the same post-commit ship publication path as undocking. Launch placement is a server choice tangent to the sampled host using both hull radii, not a claimed retail offset.
  - Contracts do not list items from shared bays. Contract creation refuses an actor- or corporation-owned root with differently owned descendants before escrow; homogeneous contracts keep their existing behavior. Repackaging also refuses mixed-owner children. Ownership transfers preserve item attributes and blueprints and apply existing SKIN cleanup; insurance policy ownership is not rewritten. Static nontradability still applies, but instance soul binding is not implemented.
  - Mining deliveries route ore to the general mining hold, then the asteroid hold, ice to the ice hold, and gas to the gas hold, falling back to cargo when the appropriate hold is unavailable. Ammunition can be reloaded from the ammo hold and unloaded to it when requested by the client. Charges reserved during a reload still occupy capacity in their original ship hold or ordinary source container until completion or return. A return exchanges its exact reservation for the charges atomically, so a valid original box being full or overfull alone does not refuse it. If the source is missing or has changed owner, the existing fallback must have real room; otherwise the reservation remains. When one return group combines reservations matching the destination with fallback arrivals, only the matching reservations are credited and the rest require room, with one deposit and notice for the group.
- **Direct trades.** Two online pilots docked in the same station can trade ISK and items from their personal hangars, including partial stacks and containers or inactive ships with their contents. Both must accept the exact offer, and any change resets both acceptances; the exchange then happens in one transaction. Cancelling, leaving the offer unchanged for `trade.idle_timeout_secs` (30 minutes by default), disconnecting, undocking, clone jumping or a server restart returns the offered items to where they came from. Offered items cannot be moved, split, stacked, renamed or trashed until the trade ends.
- **Dogma.** Effective attributes are computed on the server from the character, skills, hull, modules, charges, drones, and the implants and boosters in the character's own slots, including stacking penalties. Passive skill, implant and booster bonuses are also sent to the client as its character brain, so the fitting window and the HUD show them; the brain is sent again whenever skills, implants or boosters change. Module activation goes through one registry of SDE effect IDs and one activation cycle, shared by weapons, local repairs, propulsion and mining. Modifiers the client defines only in Python are added at start to effects whose SDE modifier list is empty. Fitting follows the client's rules: slots and hardpoints, calibration, rig size, subsystems, hull and group restrictions, the most modules of a group that may be fitted, online or active at once, and required skills. A module that needs more CPU or powergrid than the ship has left can be fitted, but not brought online. When a ship's load goes above its CPU or powergrid output, for example after a skill is removed or extracted, an implant is destroyed or a booster expires, through a `/dogma` override, or when a ship fitted before these checks is boarded, modules go offline in the client's order until the load fits. See [Effects](effects.md) for the registry, what is supported, and how to add an effect family.
  - Supported module cycles, including siege, triage and cynosural modules, consume their effective data-specified fuel each cycle; this does not add unsupported effect families.
- **Space.** Undock and dock, approach, align, orbit and follow, warp, and stargate jumps. A pilot can also fly to a point in space, and fly to, align to or warp to a bookmark of their own or one in a shared folder they may use; the server reads the bookmark's stored position, refuses an expired one or one in another system, and never uses a position sent by the client. The autopilot warps only to celestials and other objects. A pilot can board an unoccupied assembled ship they own whose surface is within 6,550 m of their ship's, from a capsule or another ship; the ship they leave stays in space, stopped and unpiloted, with its own fitting, cargo and condition, and is still there after a restart. Each solar system runs as its own actor, so systems advance independently and never block each other. Ships move on the client's one-second destiny tick: a command is stamped with the tick the system is on and takes effect before the next step, as on the client. Go-to and align, the slow-down on arrival, approach, follow, orbit, stop, missile flight, and warp alignment and flight use carbonengine destiny's mechanics and integration, so other pilots see the same course. Ball collisions are off by default (`space.collisions`), because the server has no collision shapes: ships pass through each other and through stations, gates and asteroids, on the server and in the client. With them on, ships bump off each other and off stations, gates and asteroids with destiny's sphere collision. Missiles fly and hit the same either way. A ship undocks at the mouth of one of the station's exit channels for its size and flies out along it, as the client places it; with collisions on, a mouth inside the station's sphere is moved out to its surface. Gate arrival and GM teleports place a ship outside the sphere of the object it arrives at. A stargate jump plays the jump tunnel during its four-second wait, before the ship moves to the next system. The ship arrives cloaked, as does a ship whose pilot logs in while in space; the cloak drops, visibly for every pilot on grid, when the pilot moves, stops, warps, activates a module or starts locking a target, or after 60 seconds. Docking is accepted only when the ship's surface is within 2500 m of the station's; the ship then stops, and enters the station 5.5 seconds after the client is told the request was accepted. Belt rats and the contents of a scan site appear when the ship actually lands from warp. Timers and NPCs run every 100 ms and read positions interpolated between ticks, as the client draws them. A per-system pilot cap with a jump queue is optional.
  Warp requests use each pilot's effective fitted base warp speed and multiplier, including supported implant bonuses. Fleet members use their own fits; the chosen speed stays fixed for that trip.
  Ordinary warp consumes capacitor once at actual activation, using the current fitted warp capacitor need, ship mass and stand-off-adjusted distance. If energy is insufficient, each ship travels its own proportional distance; an empty positive-cost ship stops. A zero need permits the full trip without a debit. Fleet destination callbacks run once for the first full arrival, not a shortened arrival.
- **Combat.**
  - Targeting, ammunition, weapon groups, turrets and missiles, and capacitor.
  - Weapon groups: online guns of the same type and ammunition can be linked into a bank in slot order, and banks can be merged, peeled, unlinked and destroyed. A bank fires and reloads as one, and activating a slave fires its master's bank. Taking any member offline destroys its bank; dragging a fitted gun out of its slot removes it from its bank, or destroys the bank if it is the master. Bank membership is saved; a firing cycle is not.
  - Turret hit chance follows carbonengine destiny's accuracy formula, for ships and NPCs: angular velocity against tracking and signature, and surface distance against optimal range and falloff. Missile damage is scaled by the target's signature radius and speed, at the moment the missile hits, against the missile's explosion radius and velocity. NPCs with no falloff hit only inside optimal range, and NPCs with no tracking ignore angular velocity.
  - Electronic warfare from ships and NPCs: stasis webifiers, warp scramblers and disruptors, target painters, sensor dampeners, tracking and guidance disruptors, remote sensor boosters and remote tracking computers, including their scripts. A scrambled ship cannot warp, and scrambling shuts off microwarpdrives. Tracking and guidance disruption shorten an NPC's range and lower its hit chance and missile damage. Targeted ECM and burst jammers work against the target's strongest sensor type: the chance is the jammer's strength for that type against the sensor's strength, with optimal range and falloff. A jammed ship or NPC loses its locks and cannot lock again until the jam ends, after the module's jam duration or its cycle time.
    Effective warp disruption also cancels an accepted warp while it is still aligning; an already active warp continues.
  - Capacitor warfare from ships and NPCs: neutralizers, nosferatu and capacitor transfer, with optimal range and falloff. Capacitor boosters use their charges, and ancillary shield boosters and armor repairers use their charges and reload from cargo automatically when they run dry. An NPC transfers capacitor only to another NPC of its own spawn group, or with the same owner, never to its player target.
  - Remote shield, armor and hull repair from ships and NPCs, with optimal range and falloff: shield lands at the start of a cycle, armor and hull at the end, as for local repair. Ancillary remote repairers use their charges and reload from cargo. An NPC repairs the most damaged ally of its own spawn group or owner, never its target. Remote assistance, capacitor transfer included, does not give Crimewatch flags yet.
  - Drones launch from the bay and can engage, mine, return to orbit or to the bay, assist or guard a fleet member, and be abandoned or scooped. Launch count, bandwidth, bay capacity, control range and drone attributes come from the same fitting evaluator as ships, with the owner's skills. Combat, logistics and electronic-warfare drones use the same effects as modules and NPCs, and their attacks count as their owner's. Mining drones fly to the rock and bring the ore back to the hold. Aggressive idle drones answer legal attacks on their owner: with focus fire on, they all take the attacker with the lowest ball ID; otherwise idle drones share the attackers in drone-ID order. A command from the pilot always takes precedence.
  - Smartbombs, bombs and Vorton projectors. A smartbomb, fitted to a ship or an NPC, damages the ships, drones and NPCs in range around it. A bomb flies ahead of the launching ship and detonates after its flight time. A Vorton projector hits a locked target and chains to further targets in range of it, up to the fitted target count.
  - Cloaking devices with the client's activation rules and targeting delay, micro jump drives, tractor beams, cargo and ship scanners, survey scanner cycles, and automatic and passive targeting. A tractor beam pulls a wreck or container to the ship's surface. Compression modules advertise their fitted types and range.
  - Overheating: modules that support it can be overloaded one at a time, by rack, or as a weapon bank. A running module starts overloading at its next cycle and stops after its current cycle, and an idle module can be set to overload before it is activated. Overloaded cycles build rack heat, which can damage the online modules in that rack and burns a module out at its structure hit points; a paid station repair restores it. The damage chance follows EVE University's published overheating research, because the client has the rack heat curve but not the damage roll. Rack heat and overload settings are not saved: they reset on docking, jumping, logging off, leaving the ship and restart, and warp keeps them. Resetting heat on docking is a server policy; on retail, rack heat keeps cooling while docked.
  - Salvagers: the chance is the wreck's access difficulty against the salvager's bonus. A successful salvage claims the wreck once and leaves its loot in it. Wreck yield tables do not ship with the client, so it gives `salvage.salvage_material_stacks` stacks of a random published salvaged material instead (see [Configuration](configuration.md)).
  - NPC AI, bounties, wrecks and loot.
  - Crimewatch: timers, criminal flags, security status, CONCORD and killmails.
  - Player bounties on characters, corporations and alliances, with contributions, leaderboards and search. A pilot who destroys a target's ship is paid from the matching bounty pools, at most 20% of the destroyed value, once per killmail.
  - Kill rights: illegal aggression earns the victim an expiring right against the aggressor, which the victim can activate or sell to anyone or to one pilot. Activating it makes the aggressor a suspect anyone may shoot; the right is used up when the aggressor dies while it is active.
- **Skills.** A persistent training queue completes through scheduled jobs, including after a restart. Skill history and attribute remaps are saved, and a saved queue is validated as the client does. Skillbooks, free skill points, injectors and extractors can be used, and large and small injectors can be split or combined; item use and the skill-point change commit together. The daily Alpha skill injector works only on an Alpha account, once per character in any 24 hours, and the client is told when the character can use one again. Skills can also be bought directly from the skill catalogue, alone or with the buy-and-inject button: a skill on the static list of skills for direct purchase costs its base price plus 30%, rounded to the cent, and is injected at level 0 without trained prerequisites. A batch is bought in one transaction; any refusal leaves the wallet and the skills unchanged. Skill plans are served through the public gateway.
- **Personal skill plans.** A pilot can keep up to ten plans, each with an ordered sequence of up to 150 skill levels and five milestones. Plans can be shared for viewing; milestone progress belongs to the viewer and uses trained levels, not expert-system grants. A personal or certified plan can be tracked, or tracking can be cancelled; the choice survives a restart.
  Skill prerequisites use the native static prerequisite graph. A level-zero prerequisite is retained and never blocks; a level-zero plan step is a marker, not a trained level.
  Cyclic prerequisite closures are retained in static data but cannot be used for new plan steps or milestone targets. Existing cyclic targets are reported in the startup audit and remain invalid and incomplete.
- **Expert systems.** Consuming an expert system item the pilot can reach installs it on the character for its duration, at most three at a time. An installed one can be extended by its duration once 30 days or less remain, and removed with no refund; a hidden or retired one can still be installed from an existing item but not extended. While installed it grants its skills: a skill's effective level is the higher of the trained and the granted level, and effective levels apply to fitting, modules, the market, corporations and the client's skill lists, while training and skill points use trained levels only. Expiry runs on the same durable timeline as training and boosters, including after a restart: modules that lose a prerequisite stop, and ships, roles and clones are kept. Mining Barge Operations and Brand Manager can be bought for PLEX in the New Eden Store's expert system category, also as a gift to another account. A purchase goes to the account's redeeming queue; redeeming creates the item, and only consuming it installs or extends the system. The client shows expert systems unless `expert_systems.enabled` is turned off (see [Configuration](configuration.md)).
- **Implants and boosters.** Implants can be plugged in and boosters consumed from the pilot's own hangar or active ship. They apply to fitting and character attributes, and a booster's rolled side effects are saved with it. Booster expiry and skill training completion run on one durable timeline per character, including after a restart, so points trained before a booster expires are counted at the old rate.
- **Jump clones.** Each jump clone keeps the implants plugged into it, with their item IDs and attributes. A pilot docked at an NPC station with a cloning service or a jump clone facility can install an empty clone there, one per station, up to the number their skills allow; rename it; destroy it with its implants; and jump to a clone in another station or swap into one in the same station. The body left behind keeps its implants. Implants marked to follow jump clones move with the pilot, and boosters are never stored in a spare body. Each install and each jump costs `clones.service_fee_isk` (900,000 ISK by default), paid to the NPC station. The jump cooldown comes from the character's attributes; a same-station swap pays the fee but does not start the cooldown. When a jump would destroy the spare clone already at the departure station, the client asks first. After a jump, training rates and the character brain follow the new body, and skill extraction keeps the skills that implants in spare clones depend on.
- **Economy.**
  - Character and corporation wallet balances use exact cent arithmetic across the signed 36-digit cent domain; balances themselves cannot be negative. Wallet journal amounts are exact, and the wider 37-digit cent balance snapshot preserves intermediate gross sale proceeds. Client wallet and journal values are projected with the applicable personal or corporation display cap; those projections do not limit the authoritative balance. GM `/setisk` preserves exact decimal balances; `/addisk` accepts a signed exact adjustment and refuses a negative resulting balance.
  - PLEX vault transfers: a pilot docked at an NPC station can withdraw PLEX to a real stack in their personal hangar for market use and return it to the account vault. The vault change and item mutation commit together; a failed later sale leaves the stack available for recovery. Direct-trade withdrawal and structure transfers are not supported.
  - Regional market: seeded NPC orders, escrow, taxes and fees, history. Each participant in a personal trade has its own wallet account, and the market's cash movements go into the wallet journal in the same transaction as the money: immediate purchases, gross sale proceeds and sales tax, broker fees, and escrow deposits, top-ups and refunds. A sale still credits its net amount once and an order still debits its combined amount once; the journal splits that one change into its parts exactly and leaves out parts that are zero. Buying through an already funded buy order shows in the market transactions, not as a second debit. The journal shows the last 30 days or a calendar month (UTC), with entry labels from the static data. Journal amounts and balances sent to the client are clamped to the signed personal or corporation wallet limit; stored values remain unchanged.
    Personal and corporation orders for ordinary types share the NPC-station regional book and matcher with NPC liquidity. PLEX instead has one player-only global book across regions, with global best quotes and actual trade history. Seeded NPC orders for ordinary types use the UTC day anchored to seeding time as their issue date.
    The issuer, financial owner and asset owner are distinct. Corporate trading requires current trade authority and Take for the selected wallet division; selling also requires Take for the actual office hangar division. Personal assets cannot be sold as corporate assets or vice versa: moving them between owners is a separate inventory operation. Only the original issuer, still authorized for the original division, can modify or cancel an order; authorized managers can read other issuers' orders but cannot act on them. Orders for unknown types, types without a market group or non-tradable types are refused. Immediate purchases and sales, including every `BuyMultipleItems` row, accept any whole-cent limit price from 0.01 ISK up to the client's maximum order price and fill at the resting orders' prices within that limit; an order that rests and a new price for an existing order must also be on the client's four-significant-figure price tick.

    Personal PLEX purchases and unsold PLEX refunds go to the original account vault. Selling starts with a real physical stack: withdrawal from the vault and the subsequent sale are two separate transactions, so a failed sale leaves the withdrawn stack in the hangar. The sale does not debit the vault again. Corporate PLEX is physical: purchases go to Deliveries at the fill station and unsold PLEX returns to the original valid office division, or Deliveries at that station if custody no longer permits the original return. The original corporation and wallet division remain responsible after membership or authority changes.

    Initial non-immediate orders pay broker fees on the full original notional, and high-security orders also pay security tax on that notional, journalled as reference type 194; sales tax applies to fills. A same-price modification is a no-op; repricing is subject to the configured cooldown, and an order at or past expiry cannot be repriced. Cancellation and expiry return remaining escrow or assets; scheduled expiry resumes after restart. Ordinary and dedicated PLEX modification methods refuse orders of the other scope.

    Corporate orders retain their original corporation, wallet division and sell source even if the issuer goes offline, leaves or loses authority. Purchases arrive in corporation Deliveries at the actual fill station, without an office there. Unsold items return to the original office and flag while still under corporate custody, otherwise to Deliveries at the same station. Identical financial accounts do not match each other; different divisions of the same corporation may trade. Issuer order history and quota include corporate orders, while personal escrow excludes them. Corporate transaction reads expose only currently readable divisions and identify the actual account side; a cross-division fill may appear once for each readable side.

    `BuyMultipleItems` accepts at most 100 rows under one market lock and transaction: a refused child rolls back the entire batch. Each accepted immediate child has a unique order ID and history entry, with its actual unfilled remainder in the response and Expiry notice, including partial or zero fills. Multi-sell children instead reauthorize and commit independently; successful children remain committed if others fail, with an existing `CustomNotify` summary for multiple items and the original error for a single item.

    Wallet and journal postings, market prices and escrow use checked exact cents, and PLEX vault and escrow quantities are whole units; client projections do not change stored values. Gross proceeds, net credit and tax are conserved. Journal captions distinguish the authorizing actor from the actual financial account as appropriate to the reference type. Order, balance and item notifications are sent after commit to currently authorized division or custody readers. A crash can lose these ephemeral pushes; reload or relog recovers persisted state, not an exactly-once network response or a private retry queue. Outstanding corporate orders prevent final dissolution, and outstanding personally owned orders prevent character deletion; corporate orders merely issued by that character are not personal obligations.
  - Direct skill purchases: each charged skill has its own wallet journal entry, committed together with learning the skills; free skills add no journal entry.
  - Contracts: item exchange, auction and courier, for pilots and for corporations. A public contract costs the broker's fee and a deposit when it is created, as the creation window shows them; an item exchange also pays its sales tax then, and an auction pays sales tax on the price it sells for. A private contract costs only the minimum broker's fee. The deposit comes back when someone takes the contract, and is lost if it is deleted or expires untaken. A courier contract can carry at most 1,200,000 m³, the client's limit, and a refused contract charges nothing. A corporation contract is created, accepted or bid on from a wallet division the member may spend from, and fees, refunds and settlement use that same division; its items come from an office division the member may take from, or from the station's Deliveries hangar. Items a corporation receives, including the items it asked for in an exchange, arrive in Deliveries, and returned items go there too when their office no longer belongs to the corporation. Requested items are handed over as they are, so a blueprint copy, an assembled ship or a container with its contents arrives unchanged. Blueprints of installed or ready industry jobs cannot go into a contract or be handed over. An overdue courier stays in progress until someone fails it, and can still be delivered. A repeated or out-of-date action on a contract changes nothing, and a failed auction settlement is retried. A game master can expire an outstanding contract from the contract window: an auction with a bid settles at once, and any other contract stays reclaimable by its issuer, as after a natural expiry.
  - Industry: manufacturing, research, copying and invention at NPC stations, for pilots and for corporations. A corporation job takes its blueprint and materials from office divisions the installer may take from, delivers into the chosen division, and is paid from the installer's wallet or from a corporation division they may spend from. A job becomes ready at its end time, including after a restart, and is delivered when the pilot asks; until then its blueprint cannot be moved, trashed, sold or traded. Station taxes and the system cost index come from the industry configuration, the station's tax settings are read-only, and a job whose quoted cost or tax no longer matches is refused.
  - Mutaplasmids: applying a mutaplasmid to a compatible module or drone consumes both and creates one mutated item. Each mutated attribute is drawn uniformly from the mutaplasmid's range, and the result keeps the source's own attributes with those factors applied, so fitting and Show Info use them, including after a restart. The inputs may be in the station hangar, the active ship or an owned container there; an item in a corporation office hangar also needs the division's take role. Mutaplasmid ranges and compatible types come from the imported static data, and a mutaplasmid with missing or malformed data is refused.
- **Agents.** Kill missions, epic arcs, NPC standings, loyalty points and LP stores. LP store offers come from a catalogue the operator imports from ESI with `lp-import` (see [LP offer import](lp-import.md)); the server itself never contacts ESI. While the catalogue is empty, every NPC corporation offers its race's faction items, priced from retail values, and the server logs a warning at start. You can buy an offer for yourself or, with withdrawal access to the chosen wallet division, for your corporation, which then pays the LP and ISK; the required items always come from your hangar in the station. The agent finder and the agent window show which agents have epic arc missions for the character: an arc mission from that agent is offered or accepted, or the character's arc progress unlocks a next step with them.
- **Exploration.** Anomalies and signatures, probe and directional scanning, wormholes, the data and relic hacking minigame, and belt rats. An account with the QA role can scan the sites it selects in its current system to full certainty; their signatures then count as scanned for that character, as after an ordinary scan.
- **Star map.** The jumps, ship kills, NPC kills, pod kills and visited-systems filters show what happened on the server. A gate or wormhole arrival counts one jump for the system the pilot arrives in, and one visit to it for that pilot; leaving a system, failed jumps and GM teleports do not count. Player ship and capsule losses, NPC deaths and the destruction of unpiloted ships count once each in their system; drones do not count. The filters cover the last hour or the last 24 hours, and a pilot's visits are kept for good. If the server stops right after an arrival or an NPC death, that event can be left uncounted. Station information (type, owner, operation and location), a solar system's NPC faction and the list of stations an NPC corporation owns, which the LP store uses to find the nearest one, come from the static data.
- **Mining.** Asteroid belts, ore, ice and gas, mining modules, and belt regrowth. The personal mining ledger lists every delivery from mining modules and drones, with the residue wasted; the window shows new entries when the client next reloads it. An active ship compressor lets its pilot and the pilot's fleet compress items in space: a stack in the active ship's cargo or mining holds, of a type the compressor's fitted lists accept and within its range measured surface to surface, becomes the same number of units of its compressed type in the same hold.
  - Each solar system keeps its own asteroid fields and rocks. Loading a system restores its fields and finishes any belt regrowth that is due before pilots see it, and scanners, targeting and mining all see the same rocks. Mined quantities, depletion and regrowth deadlines survive a restart.
- **Social.** Chat (local, corporation, fleet and other channels), fleets, EVE mail and notifications, the calendar, and bookmarks. The server decides which chat channels a pilot may join: local, corporation, alliance, faction and fleet channels follow the pilot's current session, whatever room name the client asks for. Pilots can create their own channels, which only members can join unless the owner makes them public, and private chats between two pilots. Owners and managers invite members, set the message of the day, mute and ban, for a set time or permanently, and delete the channel; channels, their members and moderation survive a restart. Fleets have wings and squads, member options (free move, the automatic squad, the size limit and each member's consent to fleet warps), bulk moves and wing arrangements by the leader, and the fleet finder: the server decides who may see and apply to an advert from current affiliations, the leader's contacts and blocks and the applicant's security status, and an accepted application becomes a normal invitation. Fleet, system and bubble broadcasts follow the fleet hierarchy. Members on the watchlist report their damage to the watcher anywhere in space. A commander's fleet warp takes every member under them in the same system who accepts fleet warps, each through their own ship's warp and scramble checks. A member who disconnects or logs off keeps their seat for two minutes by default, and the client rejoins it automatically after logging back in; until then the fleet does not notify, warp or count them, and a leaderless fleet passes the lead to a connected member. Fleets do not survive a restart. Personal contacts: pilots, corporations, alliances and agents with a standing and a watchlist flag, and a separate block list; adding or editing a contact can notify the other pilot. Contacts show as online or offline, and pilots on a watchlist announce when they log on or off. Bookmark folders are personal or shared. In the access lists a pilot creates groups of pilots, corporations, alliances or everyone, each entry a member, a manager, an admin or excluded; an excluded entry wins, otherwise the most specific entry decides, and a group's last admin cannot be removed. A shared folder names a group for each of four roles: view reads and copies its bookmarks, use adds bookmarks and subfolders and edits the pilot's own, manage edits, moves and deletes any of them, and admin changes the folder and its groups. Pilots add shared folders to their known folders, and open clients update as soon as a folder changes or a pilot loses access; groups, folders and known folders survive a restart. An item filter or the fleet broadcast settings can be shared as a link in chat or mail; any signed-in player who opens the link loads a copy of them as they were when it was made. Pilots can create mailing lists and join them; the owner and operators manage members, mute or kick them, set who may join by character, corporation or alliance, and keep a welcome mail that each new member gets once. Mail to a list reaches its members at the time it is sent, online or offline, and a deleted list keeps its name on the mail already sent to it.
  - Private system replies use the client's reserved `admin` transport occupant without a player profile; the displayed speaker remains owner 1. Presence is kept only while the recipient's chat connection or room is active, and replies are not retained for offline delivery.
  - Fleet location queries show online, visible members in the same system. Fleet commanders (the fleet leader, wing commanders and squad commanders) can set or clear visible target tags for fleet members; tags are process-local and do not survive a restart. A commander can regroup the fleet: subordinates in the same system who allow regrouping fly towards the commander, without warping. Fleet warp also works to a bookmark.
- **Corporations and alliances.**
  - Player corporations: founding, applications and invitations, and bulletins, contacts and labels. A personnel manager accepts an application to make an offer; only the applicant's confirmation joins them to the corporation. Invitations also require confirmation. Application history, responses and notices to the applicant or authorized managers are saved, and a nonempty welcome mail is sent once on joining. Member capacity comes from the CEO's effective management skills and the static skill effects; it is saved at founding or when the CEO updates it. Older corporations show a computed limit until the first admission check or update saves it. Full and over-capacity corporations refuse admissions, and skill loss does not evict members. Joining clears the old roles, title, base and wallet selection and records employment. After commit, the session, member lists and counts, chat rooms and presence, and the pilot's affiliation in space are refreshed; the pilot is invited to newly available organization rooms, and chat checks current membership for access. Character search finds active players for invitations, owner search finds players and organizations, and war search finds player corporations and alliances; the general owner search also includes static NPC owners. These searches support exact and literal-substring matching.
    - Corporation roles and grantable roles are saved separately for all locations, headquarters, the member's base, and other stations. Titles contribute within the same scopes. Hangar and industry access uses the actual office station rather than the office container ID. A member can refuse new role grants. The prepare-to-leave action clears all direct and grantable roles and held titles and blocks new grants; removing only one role does not clear unrelated rights. Revoking a role takes effect for the next protected operation, including through a previously bound object.
    - Members can be queried by name, membership date, base, titles and scoped roles, with independent title-derived and implied-role options. The member list is paged in stable character order. Directors receive detailed member tracking; ordinary members receive a smaller projection without ships, locations or exact logon/logoff times.
    - Leaving a player corporation requires no remaining direct, grantable or title-derived rights, completion of the configured role-removal wait (24 hours by default), and being docked or fully offline. A director's removal request is saved when those conditions are not ready, and completes through the durable deadline or the member's next completed dock or logoff, without polling. Pending or refused members appear separately from members actually removed in the bulk result. Joining another player corporation or founding one from an existing player corporation uses the same exit rules; joining from an NPC corporation is unchanged.
    - A bulk member-removal request containing the caller is refused before any member is changed. Leaving the corporation yourself uses the single-member path instead.
    - A CEO can hand control to an eligible member, or use the client's confirmed liquidation path when there is no eligible successor. The corporation is retained as closed rather than deleting its history or assets. A last-member corporation cannot be dissolved while it remains in an alliance. Historical founders do not retain management privileges after succession.
    - Corporation profiles, recruitment switch, ISK and LP tax rates, division names and logos persist and update the existing client caches. Directors and the active CEO can change the profile and logo; only the active CEO can rename divisions. A real logo change costs 100 ISK from the corporation's master wallet, as the client shows. An identical logo costs nothing; an unaffordable change changes nothing.
    - Corporation creation clamps finite ISK and LP tax rates to the range from zero to one and refuses malformed or nonfinite rates before charging the startup cost. Later profile edits require both rates to be within that range.
    - Corporation member auditing records new membership, CEO and role transitions from deployment onward. Authorized auditors and directors can read bounded history for their own corporation; old history is not invented.
    - Corporation wallet reads and balance-update audiences use the same current membership and effective-role rules as other protected operations. Accountant and Junior Accountant provide broad read access, but spending from or selecting a division requires its Take role; Director authority inherits that right for every division. A stored CEO identity alone does not grant wallet access after Director authority is lost. A corporate withdrawal locks current corporation membership and checks withdrawal rights inside the same transaction as the debit, so a completed revocation cannot be bypassed by an earlier permission check.
    - Corporation ISK tax applies to each NPC kill bounty and agent mission reward separately only when its gross amount is above 100,000 ISK; exactly 100,000 is exempt. The tax is rounded to cents and enters the corporation's master wallet, with the member receiving the remainder. Agent mission LP rewards pay the configured LP tax independently of ISK, rounded down per reward, to the corporation under the original NPC issuer. Current membership and rates are read when the reward settles. Personal loyalty-income progress records the net LP received by the member; the corporation's tax share is not counted as a second personal reward. Daily goals, GM grants, transfers, refunds, insurance and market or contract proceeds are not taxed.
  - Seven wallet divisions with ISK transfers between characters and corporations. Reading a division's balance or journal and receiving its balance changes need the same right: accountants, junior accountants, directors and the active CEO with Director authority can read every division, and other members need that division's take role. Offices rented per station, with corporation hangars and rent bills that can be paid automatically. Directors, the CEO, accountants, junior accountants and traders can see each station's Deliveries hangar, and all of them except junior accountants can take items from it; nothing can be put into it.
  - Shares, dividends and votes. A corporation is founded holding all of its own shares (`corporations.initial_shares`, 1000 by default); corporations that existed before this version receive them once at the next start, and changing the setting later does not alter existing corporations. A director or the CEO gives the corporation's shares to pilots and other corporations, and pilots move their own. The CEO pays dividends from the master wallet division to every holder except the corporation itself, each rounded down to the cent, with the remainder kept. A director or the CEO opens general votes; each ballot weighs the shares its pilot held, or held through a corporation they lead, when the vote opened, and a holder counts once. A vote closes on time, including after a restart, and its result is the weighted total.
  - Alliances: founding, applications, member removal, executor support, and bulletins and contacts. Applications, withdrawals and executor decisions commit together with their membership changes. Acceptance takes effect immediately; offline members see their corporation's alliance when they next select their character. A rejected application can be deleted before applying again, but an effective, previously accepted or rejected application cannot be reset by applying again. A GM can force acceptance only for a new application.
  - Mutual wars: a director or the CEO of a corporation outside an alliance, or of an alliance's executor corporation for the alliance, can invite another such corporation or alliance to a war. An invitation lasts seven days and can be accepted, declined or withdrawn, and an owner can block invitations. An accepted war costs nothing and starts 24 hours later. Either side can retract it; the two stay at war for a 24-hour cooldown, and then the war ends. Between owners at war, attacks are legal and bring no criminal flag, security loss or kill right; a pilot fights as their alliance, or as their corporation outside one. Each kill report records the wars it counts for and the alliances of the victim and the final blow at that moment, so a war's report and kill statistics stay the same when pilots or corporations change affiliation. Invitations, wars and their deadlines survive a restart.
- **Store.** New Eden Store catalogue, PLEX vault, redeeming queue and seasonal login rewards. A paid purchase saves the PLEX payment, the purchase record and the redeem token together, and only then updates the balance and the redeeming queue; an invalid currency, quantity, recipient or offer, or too little PLEX, changes nothing. A gift appears in the receiving account's queue at once if it is online, or at its next login. Redeeming delivers items to the station the pilot is docked in, or to their home station, and skill points to the character, all in one step; a token that cannot be delivered stays in the queue with an error. A seasonal reward gives an Alpha account the day's Alpha item and an Omega account both the Alpha and the Omega item. In a campaign that allows it, an Omega account that claims a reward also receives the Omega items of the days it claimed as Alpha.
- **Ship SKINs.** Ordinary first-party permanent and non-single-use timed licenses can be activated from reachable owned items: a batch uses one unit from each distinct item and commits all or none. Licenses cover every compatible hull in the static data. The owner can apply a licensed SKIN or cancel an application on a reachable owned assembled ship; cancellation does not require a valid license. Transfer or repackaging clears its application, not the license. Applications persist, and visible observers receive snapshots and incremental notices. GML tools grant, remove or expire licenses for the current character. As a server policy, finite renewal adds positive days to the later of now and the stored expiry; a permanent grant replaces a finite license, while a repeated permanent grant is refused. Expiry is strict (expiry earlier than now) and converges at startup, login and query as well as through durable scheduled jobs.
- **Crates.** The crate window shows what a crate holds, for a crate type or for a crate the pilot can reach. Contents come from the static data or, where it lists none or contradicts itself, from the server's own content in `crates/server/content/crates.jsonl`, inferred from the crate's description. Random contents list every candidate, and a ranged amount shows its minimum. Ordinary direct-list crates can be opened: `OpenCrate` opens one, and `ClaimLootFromCrateStack` opens up to 30 from one stack, all or nothing, returning `(opened, requested)`; a larger stack opens 30 per call. Rewards appear immediately in the opener's personal hangar at the current station when docked, or in the active ship's cargo in space; the operation is refused if the cargo lacks room. Opening a corporation-owned crate requires hangar Take permission and gives the rewards to the opener. Source consumption, reward creation and receipts commit together, followed by notifications. `ClaimLoot` confirms a receipt only for the opener and the exact destination; it can be repeated and still succeeds after the reward is used or merged. It never creates or moves items or sends notifications (see [Known limitations](limitations.md)).
  `GetCrateLocation` checks the same source authorization and eligibility without changing or reserving anything, and returns the current reward destination. Opening independently validates the current session and inventory again.
- **Ship adaptation chips.** Published trees, inactive missions and zero-filled user chip balances are readable. Eligible physical injector stacks credit a distinct virtual chip currency in the quantity defined by their SDE dogma attributes; quoted balances can be exchanged for corporation LP using the corporation quote or dataset default. Injection notifies item then user; exchange notifies LP, user, then exchange success. Exhausted zero balances survive restart. Node unlock, tasks, QA completion and effects remain unsupported; no ISK, skills, SP or bonuses are granted. See [remote services](remote-services.md#ship-adaptation) for acquisition, client and cache limits.
- **Lua scripts.** Scripts in `crates/server/lua/` can answer remote calls and add GM commands without changes to the server's routing. A script can also intercept one exact remote call before the server answers it, for example to refuse a buy order with a message, and react to game events such as a character being selected, a ship being lost, a market order being filled or a mission being accepted. Events reach scripts in order, after the change that caused them has been saved. Scripts can also run on a timer, read many stored values in one call, run simulated pilots, and catch errors with `pcall`; running out of time or instructions cannot be caught. More script directories can be added with `lua.mod_dirs`. The bundled scripts keep a message of the day that a GM sets, sent when a character is selected, and answer five client calls that only need fixed answers. See [Lua scripts](lua.md).
