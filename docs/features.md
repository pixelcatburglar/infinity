# Features

- **Faction warfare information.** Corporation militia whitelists remain available, but direct enlistment and leaving are refused while faction warfare is closed. Empty occupation, victory-point and shipcaster information lets the dashboard open; as server policy, faction home systems from static data serve as the displayed headquarters locations, not official warzone HQ data.

- **Login and characters.** GPS handshake with the Placebo crypto pack. An account is created the first time a new user name logs in. Character selection and creation. Each account has a clone grade, Alpha or Omega, kept in the database. Accounts start as Omega with no end date; a GM can change the grade with `/clonegrade`, and an Omega grade given for a set time turns into Alpha when its grace period runs out, also across a restart. A character with no active ship, such as a new one, receives their race's rookie ship fitted with online civilian modules (a gun, a miner, an afterburner and defensive modules) and one Civilian Hobgoblin in the drone bay. The server chooses this fit from the civilian module line; it is not a copy of the live server's. Civilian guns need no ammunition. An existing ship is never refitted or restocked.
- **Stations and items.** Inventory and hangars, and the assets window with asset search. An item can be moved only where the pilot can reach both ends: when docked, the current station's hangars, the pilot's corporation office there, and the ships and containers inside them; in space, the active ship, an owned ship with a fleet hangar or ship maintenance bay nearby, and a cargo container or wreck within its operational distance. Every space check (hangar containers, cargo and other holds, contracts and courier mission cargo) counts an item at the volume the client shows: a packaged ship or module takes its packaged volume, and a courier package takes the volume of what it holds. Boarding, assembling and repackaging ships; a sealed courier package cannot be repackaged. Repackaging a fitted ship puts its modules, subsystems, drones, cargo and ammunition in the station hangar; rigs cannot be unfitted, so they are destroyed once the player confirms the client's warning, except on strategic cruisers, whose rigs go to the hangar too. Saved fittings, ship insurance, jump clones and reprocessing. Station repair: the quote covers the armor, hull and module damage of the selected items, and the ISK is paid in the same transaction as the repair. A selection without modules can be repaired in part; one that includes a module must be paid in full. Docking does not repair a ship. Private notes in the info window. The station guest list shows the other online pilots docked in the same station and updates as they arrive or leave. Crystals show the damage they have taken. A new name for a ship, container or other item shows at once wherever it is listed. Trashing removes an item together with everything inside it in one step; online modules are taken offline first, which also clears their weapon groups, pending reloads and overheating. Items in a corporation's Deliveries hangar cannot be trashed.
- **Direct trades.** Two online pilots docked in the same station can trade ISK and items from their personal hangars, including partial stacks and containers or inactive ships with their contents. Both must accept the exact offer, and any change resets both acceptances; the exchange then happens in one transaction. Cancelling, leaving the offer unchanged for `trade.idle_timeout_secs` (30 minutes by default), disconnecting, undocking, clone jumping or a server restart returns the offered items to where they came from. Offered items cannot be moved, split, stacked, renamed or trashed until the trade ends.
- **Dogma.** Effective attributes are computed on the server from the character, skills, hull, modules, charges, drones, and the implants and boosters in the character's own slots, including stacking penalties. Passive skill, implant and booster bonuses are also sent to the client as its character brain, so the fitting window and the HUD show them; the brain is sent again whenever skills, implants or boosters change. Module activation goes through one registry of SDE effect IDs and one activation cycle, shared by weapons, local repairs, propulsion and mining. Modifiers the client defines only in Python are added at start to effects whose SDE modifier list is empty. Fitting follows the client's rules: slots and hardpoints, calibration, rig size, subsystems, hull and group restrictions, the most modules of a group that may be fitted, online or active at once, and required skills. A module that needs more CPU or powergrid than the ship has left can be fitted, but not brought online. When a ship's load goes above its CPU or powergrid output, for example after a skill is removed or extracted, an implant is destroyed or a booster expires, through a `/dogma` override, or when a ship fitted before these checks is boarded, modules go offline in the client's order until the load fits. See [Effects](effects.md) for the registry, what is supported, and how to add an effect family.
- **Space.** Undock and dock, approach, align, orbit and follow, warp, and stargate jumps. A pilot can board an unoccupied assembled ship they own whose surface is within 6,550 m of their ship's, from a capsule or another ship; the ship they leave stays in space, stopped and unpiloted, with its own fitting, cargo and condition, and is still there after a restart. Each solar system runs as its own actor, so systems advance independently and never block each other. Ships move on the client's one-second destiny tick: a command is stamped with the tick the system is on and takes effect before the next step, as on the client. Go-to and align, the slow-down on arrival, approach, follow, orbit, stop, missile flight, and warp alignment and flight use carbonengine destiny's mechanics and integration, so other pilots see the same course. Ball collisions are off by default (`space.collisions`), because the server has no collision shapes: ships pass through each other and through stations, gates and asteroids, on the server and in the client. With them on, ships bump off each other and off stations, gates and asteroids with destiny's sphere collision. Missiles fly and hit the same either way. A ship undocks at the mouth of one of the station's exit channels for its size and flies out along it, as the client places it; with collisions on, a mouth inside the station's sphere is moved out to its surface. Gate arrival and GM teleports place a ship outside the sphere of the object it arrives at. A stargate jump plays the jump tunnel during its four-second wait, before the ship moves to the next system. The ship arrives cloaked, as does a ship whose pilot logs in while in space; the cloak drops, visibly for every pilot on grid, when the pilot moves, stops, warps, activates a module or starts locking a target, or after 60 seconds. Docking is accepted only when the ship's surface is within 2500 m of the station's; the ship then stops, and enters the station 5.5 seconds after the client is told the request was accepted. Belt rats and the contents of a scan site appear when the ship actually lands from warp. Timers and NPCs run every 100 ms and read positions interpolated between ticks, as the client draws them. A per-system pilot cap with a jump queue is optional.
- **Combat.**
  - Targeting, ammunition, weapon groups, turrets and missiles, and capacitor.
  - Weapon groups: online guns of the same type and ammunition can be linked into a bank in slot order, and banks can be merged, peeled, unlinked and destroyed. A bank fires and reloads as one, and activating a slave fires its master's bank. Taking any member offline destroys its bank; dragging a fitted gun out of its slot removes it from its bank, or destroys the bank if it is the master. Bank membership is saved; a firing cycle is not.
  - Turret hit chance follows carbonengine destiny's accuracy formula, for ships and NPCs: angular velocity against tracking and signature, and surface distance against optimal range and falloff. Missile damage is scaled by the target's signature radius and speed, at the moment the missile hits, against the missile's explosion radius and velocity. NPCs with no falloff hit only inside optimal range, and NPCs with no tracking ignore angular velocity.
  - Electronic warfare from ships and NPCs: stasis webifiers, warp scramblers and disruptors, target painters, sensor dampeners, tracking and guidance disruptors, remote sensor boosters and remote tracking computers, including their scripts. A scrambled ship cannot warp, and scrambling shuts off microwarpdrives. Tracking and guidance disruption shorten an NPC's range and lower its hit chance and missile damage. Targeted ECM and burst jammers work against the target's strongest sensor type: the chance is the jammer's strength for that type against the sensor's strength, with optimal range and falloff. A jammed ship or NPC loses its locks and cannot lock again until the jam ends, after the module's jam duration or its cycle time.
  - Capacitor warfare from ships and NPCs: neutralizers, nosferatu and capacitor transfer, with optimal range and falloff. Capacitor boosters use their charges, and ancillary shield boosters and armor repairers use their charges and reload from cargo automatically when they run dry. An NPC transfers capacitor only to another NPC of its own spawn group, or with the same owner, never to its player target.
  - Remote shield, armor and hull repair from ships and NPCs, with optimal range and falloff: shield lands at the start of a cycle, armor and hull at the end, as for local repair. Ancillary remote repairers use their charges and reload from cargo. An NPC repairs the most damaged ally of its own spawn group or owner, never its target. Remote assistance, capacitor transfer included, does not give Crimewatch flags yet.
  - Drones launch from the bay and can engage, mine, return to orbit or to the bay, assist or guard a fleet member, and be abandoned or scooped. Launch count, bandwidth, bay capacity, control range and drone attributes come from the same fitting evaluator as ships, with the owner's skills. Combat, logistics and electronic-warfare drones use the same effects as modules and NPCs, and their attacks count as their owner's. Mining drones fly to the rock and bring the ore back to the hold. Aggressive idle drones answer legal attacks on their owner: with focus fire on, they all take the attacker with the lowest ball ID; otherwise idle drones share the attackers in drone-ID order. A command from the pilot always takes precedence.
  - Smartbombs, bombs and Vorton projectors. A smartbomb, fitted to a ship or an NPC, damages the ships, drones and NPCs in range around it. A bomb flies ahead of the launching ship and detonates after its flight time. A Vorton projector hits a locked target and chains to further targets in range of it, up to the fitted target count.
  - Cloaking devices with the client's activation rules and targeting delay, micro jump drives, tractor beams, cargo and ship scanners, survey scanner cycles, and automatic and passive targeting. A tractor beam pulls a wreck or container to the ship's surface. Compression modules advertise their fitted types and range.
  - Overheating: modules that support it can be overloaded one at a time, by rack, or as a weapon bank. A running module starts overloading at its next cycle and stops after its current cycle, and an idle module can be set to overload before it is activated. Overloaded cycles build rack heat, which can damage the online modules in that rack and burns a module out at its structure hit points; a paid station repair restores it. The damage chance follows EVE University's published overheating research, because the client has the rack heat curve but not the damage roll. Rack heat and overload settings are not saved: they reset on docking, jumping, logging off, leaving the ship and restart, and warp keeps them. Resetting heat on docking is a server policy; on retail, rack heat keeps cooling while docked.
  - Salvagers: the chance is the wreck's access difficulty against the salvager's bonus. A successful salvage claims the wreck once and leaves its loot in it. Wreck yield tables do not ship with the client, so it gives `salvage.salvage_material_stacks` stacks of a random published salvaged material instead (see [Configuration](configuration.md)).
  - NPC AI, bounties, wrecks and loot.
  - Crimewatch: timers, criminal flags, security status, CONCORD and killmails.
  - Player bounties on characters, corporations and alliances, with contributions, leaderboards and search. A pilot who destroys a target's ship is paid from the matching bounty pools, at most 20% of the destroyed value, once per killmail.
  - Kill rights: illegal aggression earns the victim an expiring right against the aggressor, which the victim can activate or sell to anyone or to one pilot. Activating it makes the aggressor a suspect anyone may shoot; the right is used up when the aggressor dies while it is active.
- **Skills.** A persistent training queue completes through scheduled jobs, including after a restart. Skill history and attribute remaps are saved, and a saved queue is validated as the client does. Skillbooks, free skill points, injectors and extractors can be used, and large and small injectors can be split or combined; item use and the skill-point change commit together. The daily Alpha skill injector works only on an Alpha account, once per character in any 24 hours, and the client is told when the character can use one again. Skills can also be bought directly from the skill catalogue, alone or with the buy-and-inject button: a skill on the static list of skills for direct purchase costs its base price plus 30%, rounded to the cent, and is injected at level 0 without trained prerequisites. A batch is bought in one transaction; any refusal leaves the wallet and the skills unchanged. Skill plans are served through the public gateway.
- **Expert systems.** Consuming an expert system item the pilot can reach installs it on the character for its duration, at most three at a time. An installed one can be extended by its duration once 30 days or less remain, and removed with no refund; one that is no longer sold can still be installed but not extended. While installed it grants its skills: a skill's effective level is the higher of the trained and the granted level, and effective levels apply to fitting, modules, the market, corporations and the client's skill lists, while training and skill points use trained levels only. Expiry runs on the same durable timeline as training and boosters, including after a restart: modules that lose a prerequisite stop, and ships, roles and clones are kept. The client shows expert systems only with `expert_systems.enabled` set (see [Configuration](configuration.md)).
- **Implants and boosters.** Implants can be plugged in and boosters consumed from the pilot's own hangar or active ship. They apply to fitting and character attributes, and a booster's rolled side effects are saved with it. Booster expiry and skill training completion run on one durable timeline per character, including after a restart, so points trained before a booster expires are counted at the old rate.
- **Jump clones.** Each jump clone keeps the implants plugged into it, with their item IDs and attributes. A pilot docked at an NPC station with a cloning service or a jump clone facility can install an empty clone there, one per station, up to the number their skills allow; rename it; destroy it with its implants; and jump to a clone in another station or swap into one in the same station. The body left behind keeps its implants. Implants marked to follow jump clones move with the pilot, and boosters are never stored in a spare body. Each install and each jump costs `clones.service_fee_isk` (900,000 ISK by default), paid to the NPC station. The jump cooldown comes from the character's attributes; a same-station swap pays the fee but does not start the cooldown. When a jump would destroy the spare clone already at the departure station, the client asks first. After a jump, training rates and the character brain follow the new body, and skill extraction keeps the skills that implants in spare clones depend on.
- **Economy.**
  - Regional market: seeded NPC orders, escrow, taxes and fees, history.
  - Contracts: item exchange, auction and courier, for pilots and for corporations. A public contract costs the broker's fee and a deposit when it is created, as the creation window shows them; an item exchange also pays its sales tax then, and an auction pays sales tax on the price it sells for. A private contract costs only the minimum broker's fee. The deposit comes back when someone takes the contract, and is lost if it is deleted or expires untaken. A courier contract can carry at most 1,200,000 m³, the client's limit, and a refused contract charges nothing. A corporation contract is created, accepted or bid on from a wallet division the member may spend from, and fees, refunds and settlement use that same division; its items come from an office division the member may take from, or from the station's Deliveries hangar. Items a corporation receives, including the items it asked for in an exchange, arrive in Deliveries, and returned items go there too when their office no longer belongs to the corporation. Requested items are handed over as they are, so a blueprint copy, an assembled ship or a container with its contents arrives unchanged. Blueprints of installed or ready industry jobs cannot go into a contract or be handed over. An overdue courier stays in progress until someone fails it, and can still be delivered. A repeated or out-of-date action on a contract changes nothing, and a failed auction settlement is retried. A game master can expire an outstanding contract from the contract window: an auction with a bid settles at once, and any other contract stays reclaimable by its issuer, as after a natural expiry.
  - Industry: manufacturing, research, copying and invention at NPC stations, for pilots and for corporations. A corporation job takes its blueprint and materials from office divisions the installer may take from, delivers into the chosen division, and is paid from the installer's wallet or from a corporation division they may spend from. A job becomes ready at its end time, including after a restart, and is delivered when the pilot asks; until then its blueprint cannot be moved, trashed, sold or traded. Station taxes and the system cost index come from the industry configuration, the station's tax settings are read-only, and a job whose quoted cost or tax no longer matches is refused.
  - Mutaplasmids: applying a mutaplasmid to a compatible module or drone consumes both and creates one mutated item. Each mutated attribute is drawn uniformly from the mutaplasmid's range, and the result keeps the source's own attributes with those factors applied, so fitting and Show Info use them, including after a restart. The inputs may be in the station hangar, the active ship or an owned container there; an item in a corporation office hangar also needs the division's take role. Mutaplasmid ranges and compatible types come from the imported static data, and a mutaplasmid with missing or malformed data is refused.
- **Agents.** Kill missions, epic arcs, NPC standings, loyalty points and LP stores. LP store offers come from a catalogue the operator imports from ESI with `lp-import` (see [LP offer import](lp-import.md)); the server itself never contacts ESI. While the catalogue is empty, every NPC corporation offers its race's faction items, priced from retail values, and the server logs a warning at start. You can buy an offer for yourself or, with withdrawal access to the chosen wallet division, for your corporation, which then pays the LP and ISK; the required items always come from your hangar in the station. The agent finder and the agent window show which agents have epic arc missions for the character: an arc mission from that agent is offered or accepted, or the character's arc progress unlocks a next step with them.
- **Exploration.** Anomalies and signatures, probe and directional scanning, wormholes, the data and relic hacking minigame, and belt rats. An account with the QA role can scan the sites it selects in its current system to full certainty; their signatures then count as scanned for that character, as after an ordinary scan.
- **Star map.** The jumps, ship kills, NPC kills, pod kills and visited-systems filters show what happened on the server. A gate or wormhole arrival counts one jump for the system the pilot arrives in, and one visit to it for that pilot; leaving a system, failed jumps and GM teleports do not count. Player ship and capsule losses, NPC deaths and the destruction of unpiloted ships count once each in their system; drones do not count. The filters cover the last hour or the last 24 hours, and a pilot's visits are kept for good. If the server stops right after an arrival or an NPC death, that event can be left uncounted. Station information (type, owner, operation and location), a solar system's NPC faction and the list of stations an NPC corporation owns, which the LP store uses to find the nearest one, come from the static data.
- **Mining.** Asteroid belts, ore, ice and gas, mining modules, and belt regrowth. The personal mining ledger lists every delivery from mining modules and drones, with the residue wasted; the window shows new entries when the client next reloads it. An active ship compressor lets its pilot and the pilot's fleet compress items in space: a stack in the active ship's cargo or mining holds, of a type the compressor's fitted lists accept and within its range measured surface to surface, becomes the same number of units of its compressed type in the same hold.
- **Social.** Chat (local, corporation, fleet and other channels), fleets, EVE mail and notifications, the calendar, and bookmarks. The server decides which chat channels a pilot may join: local, corporation, alliance, faction and fleet channels follow the pilot's current session, whatever room name the client asks for. Pilots can create their own channels, which only members can join unless the owner makes them public, and private chats between two pilots. Owners and managers invite members, set the message of the day, mute and ban, for a set time or permanently, and delete the channel; channels, their members and moderation survive a restart. Fleets have wings and squads, member options (free move, the automatic squad, the size limit and each member's consent to fleet warps), bulk moves and wing arrangements by the leader, and the fleet finder: the server decides who may see and apply to an advert from current affiliations, the leader's contacts and blocks and the applicant's security status, and an accepted application becomes a normal invitation. Fleet, system and bubble broadcasts follow the fleet hierarchy. Members on the watchlist report their damage to the watcher anywhere in space. A commander's fleet warp takes every member under them in the same system who accepts fleet warps, each through their own ship's warp and scramble checks. A member who disconnects or logs off keeps their seat for two minutes by default, and the client rejoins it automatically after logging back in; until then the fleet does not notify, warp or count them, and a leaderless fleet passes the lead to a connected member. Fleets do not survive a restart. Personal contacts: pilots, corporations, alliances and agents with a standing and a watchlist flag, and a separate block list; adding or editing a contact can notify the other pilot. Contacts show as online or offline, and pilots on a watchlist announce when they log on or off. Bookmark folders are personal or shared. In the access lists a pilot creates groups of pilots, corporations, alliances or everyone, each entry a member, a manager, an admin or excluded; an excluded entry wins, otherwise the most specific entry decides, and a group's last admin cannot be removed. A shared folder names a group for each of four roles: view reads and copies its bookmarks, use adds bookmarks and subfolders and edits the pilot's own, manage edits, moves and deletes any of them, and admin changes the folder and its groups. Pilots add shared folders to their known folders, and open clients update as soon as a folder changes or a pilot loses access; groups, folders and known folders survive a restart. An item filter or the fleet broadcast settings can be shared as a link in chat or mail; any signed-in player who opens the link loads a copy of them as they were when it was made. Pilots can create mailing lists and join them; the owner and operators manage members, mute or kick them, set who may join by character, corporation or alliance, and keep a welcome mail that each new member gets once. Mail to a list reaches its members at the time it is sent, online or offline, and a deleted list keeps its name on the mail already sent to it.
  - Fleet location queries show online, visible members in the same system. Fleet commanders (the fleet leader, wing commanders and squad commanders) can set or clear visible target tags for fleet members; tags are process-local and do not survive a restart.
- **Corporations and alliances.**
  - Player corporations: founding, applications and invitations, roles and titles, a bounty tax paid into the corporation wallet, and bulletins, contacts and labels. A personnel manager accepts an application to make an offer; only the applicant's confirmation joins them to the corporation. Invitations also require confirmation. Application history, responses and notices to the applicant or authorized managers are saved, and a nonempty welcome mail is sent once on joining. Member capacity comes from the CEO's effective management skills and the static skill effects; it is saved at founding or when the CEO updates it. Older corporations show a computed limit until the first admission check or update saves it. Full and over-capacity corporations refuse admissions, and skill loss does not evict members. Joining clears the old roles, title, base and wallet selection and records employment. After commit, the session, member lists and counts, chat rooms and presence, and the pilot's affiliation in space are refreshed; the pilot is invited to newly available organization rooms, and chat checks current membership for access. Character search finds active players for invitations, owner search finds players and organizations, and war search finds player corporations and alliances; the general owner search also includes static NPC owners. These searches support exact and literal-substring matching.
  - Seven wallet divisions with ISK transfers between characters and corporations. Offices rented per station, with corporation hangars and rent bills that can be paid automatically. Directors, the CEO, accountants, junior accountants and traders can see each station's Deliveries hangar, and all of them except junior accountants can take items from it; nothing can be put into it.
  - Shares, dividends and votes. A corporation is founded holding all of its own shares (`corporations.initial_shares`, 1000 by default); corporations that existed before this version receive them once at the next start, and changing the setting later does not alter existing corporations. A director or the CEO gives the corporation's shares to pilots and other corporations, and pilots move their own. The CEO pays dividends from the master wallet division to every holder except the corporation itself, each rounded down to the cent, with the remainder kept. A director or the CEO opens general votes; each ballot weighs the shares its pilot held, or held through a corporation they lead, when the vote opened, and a holder counts once. A vote closes on time, including after a restart, and its result is the weighted total.
  - Alliances: founding, applications, member removal, executor support, and bulletins and contacts. Applications, withdrawals and executor decisions commit together with their membership changes. Acceptance takes effect immediately; offline members see their corporation's alliance when they next select their character. A rejected application can be deleted before applying again, but an effective, previously accepted or rejected application cannot be reset by applying again. A GM can force acceptance only for a new application.
  - Mutual wars: a director or the CEO of a corporation outside an alliance, or of an alliance's executor corporation for the alliance, can invite another such corporation or alliance to a war. An invitation lasts seven days and can be accepted, declined or withdrawn, and an owner can block invitations. An accepted war costs nothing and starts 24 hours later. Either side can retract it; the two stay at war for a 24-hour cooldown, and then the war ends. Between owners at war, attacks are legal and bring no criminal flag, security loss or kill right; a pilot fights as their alliance, or as their corporation outside one. Each kill report records the wars it counts for and the alliances of the victim and the final blow at that moment, so a war's report and kill statistics stay the same when pilots or corporations change affiliation. Invitations, wars and their deadlines survive a restart.
- **Store.** New Eden Store catalogue, PLEX vault, redeeming queue and seasonal login rewards. Redeeming delivers items to the station the pilot is docked in, or to their home station, and skill points to the character, all in one step; a token that cannot be delivered stays in the queue with an error. A seasonal reward gives an Alpha account the day's Alpha item and an Omega account both the Alpha and the Omega item. In a campaign that allows it, an Omega account that claims a reward also receives the Omega items of the days it claimed as Alpha.
- **Ship SKINs.** Ordinary first-party permanent and non-single-use timed licenses can be activated from reachable owned items: a batch uses one unit from each distinct item and commits all or none. Licenses cover every compatible hull in the static data. The owner can apply a licensed SKIN or cancel an application on a reachable owned assembled ship; cancellation does not require a valid license. Transfer or repackaging clears its application, not the license. Applications persist, and visible observers receive snapshots and incremental notices. GML tools grant, remove or expire licenses for the current character. As a server policy, finite renewal adds positive days to the later of now and the stored expiry; a permanent grant replaces a finite license, while a repeated permanent grant is refused. Expiry is strict (expiry earlier than now) and converges at startup, login and query as well as through durable scheduled jobs.
- **Crates.** The crate window shows what a crate holds, for a crate type or for a crate the pilot can reach. Contents come from the static data or, where it lists none or contradicts itself, from the server's own content in `crates/server/content/crates.jsonl`, inferred from the crate's description. Random contents list every candidate, and a ranged amount shows its minimum. Ordinary direct-list crates can be opened: `OpenCrate` opens one, and `ClaimLootFromCrateStack` opens up to 30 from one stack, all or nothing, returning `(opened, requested)`; a larger stack opens 30 per call. Rewards appear immediately in the opener's personal hangar at the current station when docked, or in the active ship's cargo in space; the operation is refused if the cargo lacks room. Opening a corporation-owned crate requires hangar Take permission and gives the rewards to the opener. Source consumption, reward creation and receipts commit together, followed by notifications. `ClaimLoot` confirms a receipt only for the opener and the exact destination; it can be repeated and still succeeds after the reward is used or merged. It never creates or moves items or sends notifications (see [Known limitations](limitations.md)).
- **Lua scripts.** Scripts in `crates/server/lua/` can answer remote calls and add GM commands without changes to the server's routing. A script can also intercept one exact remote call before the server answers it, for example to refuse a buy order with a message, and react to game events such as a character being selected, a ship being lost, a market order being filled or a mission being accepted. Events reach scripts in order, after the change that caused them has been saved. Scripts can also run on a timer, read many stored values in one call, run simulated pilots, and catch errors with `pcall`; running out of time or instructions cannot be caught. More script directories can be added with `lua.mod_dirs`. The bundled scripts keep a message of the day that a GM sets, sent when a character is selected, and answer five client calls that only need fixed answers. See [Lua scripts](lua.md).
- **GM slash commands** (see [GM commands](gm-commands.md)).
